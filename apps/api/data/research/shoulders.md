## Push Press
*Sources: Biomechanical analysis — push press uses leg drive to initiate the press, reducing deltoid time-under-tension in the bottom portion but allowing heavier loads at lockout; triceps and traps work harder at lockout due to supramaximal loading relative to strict press; quadriceps and glutes contribute in the dip-drive phase. No direct EMG comparison to strict press exists, but the movement pattern shifts relative contribution from delts to lower body in the initial phase and to lockout muscles at the top.*

## Bradford Press
*Sources: Campos et al. 2022 — front press favors anterior delt and pec, back press favors medial and posterior delt; Bradford press alternates between front and behind-neck positions without lockout, maintaining continuous tension on all three deltoid heads; no lockout eliminates triceps dominance at top. Practical EMG inference from the front vs back press data: all three delt heads are loaded through alternating planes with no rest at top.*

## Z Press
*Sources: Saeterbakken & Fimland 2013 — seated pressing without back support increases core demand; standing > seated for deltoid EMG, but removing back support while seated forces spinal erector and core engagement to compensate; Z press (floor-seated, no back support) removes all lower body contribution and increases core stability demand vs standard seated press. Deltoid activation similar to seated OHP but core demands significantly elevated.*

## Dumbbell Lu Raise
*Sources: The Lu raise combines a lateral raise with external rotation at the top (arms form a "goal post"). Biomechanically, the abduction phase loads lateral deltoid (per Franke et al. 2020) while the external rotation at the top loads infraspinatus and teres minor (per Reinold et al. 2004 — ER exercises produce 62% MVIC infraspinatus); the combination uniquely challenges both the lateral deltoid and external rotators through a single movement arc. Upper traps stabilize overhead position.*

## Dumbbell W Raise

## Bus Driver
*Sources: The bus driver (plate held at arm's length, rotating like a steering wheel) places the deltoids under sustained isometric hold in shoulder flexion while adding rotational demand; anterior deltoid maintains the hold position; rotator cuff muscles (infraspinatus, subscapularis) activate to control internal and external rotation under load; forearm pronators/supinators control the plate rotation. Isometric shoulder flexion hold + rotation = unique loading pattern.*

## ROTATOR CUFF EXERCISES

## Dumbbell External Rotation
*Sources: Reinold et al. 2004 (JOSPT) — sidelying external rotation produced greatest EMG: infraspinatus 62% MVIC, teres minor 67% MVIC; supraspinatus relatively low at sidelying (desirable for isolated RC work); posterior deltoid moderate activation; Sugiura et al. 2022 — distinct regions of infraspinatus (superior, middle, inferior) all activated during standing ER exercises; sidelying position provides gravitational advantage for isolated RC loading*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Rotator Cuff | maximum |
| Rear Delts | partial |
| Lateral Delts | partial |

## Cable External Rotation
*Sources: Reinold et al. 2004 — standing ER at 0° abduction: infraspinatus 36% MVIC, teres minor 41% MVIC (lower than sidelying due to gravitational loading differences); standing ER at 90° abduction: infraspinatus 53% MVIC, supraspinatus 82% MVIC, middle deltoid 87% MVIC (significantly higher deltoid involvement at 90°); cable at 0° abduction (standard rehab position) isolates RC effectively with minimal deltoid involvement*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Rotator Cuff | maximum |
| Rear Delts | partial |
| Lateral Delts | partial |

## Cable Internal Rotation
*Sources: Ha et al. 2015 (PMC4595918) — IR at 0° abduction: subscapularis primary, low deltoid activation (49.7%); IR at 90° abduction: significantly higher activation of all deltoid heads and rotator cuff (346.4%); pectoralis major and latissimus dorsi serve as powerful internal rotators assisting subscapularis; Decker et al. 1999 — strong EMG-force correlation in subscapularis (r2=0.95) during internal rotation*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Rotator Cuff | maximum |
| Front Delts | partial |
| Upper Chest | partial |

*Sources: The Cuban press is a compound movement combining upright row + external rotation + overhead press; upright row phase activates lateral deltoid and upper traps (McAllister et al. 2013); external rotation phase loads infraspinatus/teres minor (Reinold et al. 2004 — 53-62% MVIC infraspinatus); overhead press phase activates anterior deltoid and triceps; all three deltoid heads are loaded across the three phases; rotator cuff muscles are the unique focus due to the ER component under load*

## NECK EXERCISES

## Neck Curl
*Sources: Lecompte et al. 2007 — neck flexion: sternocleidomastoid (SCM) is primary mover; EMG output approximately 66% higher in flexion than extension for a given force level; Ylinen et al. 2003 — SCM activity significantly increased at every angle during flexion; deep cervical flexors (longus colli, longus capitis) assist as synergists but are not measurable with surface EMG; scalenes contribute as secondary flexors*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Neck | maximum |
| Upper Abs | partial |

## Neck Extension
*Sources: Lecompte et al. 2007 — neck extension: splenius capitis and semispinalis capitis are primary extensors; upper trapezius contributes significantly as cervical extensor; EMG relatively lower than flexion for equivalent force output (extension is mechanically stronger); Ylinen et al. 2003 — normalized EMG of semispinalis capitis and upper trapezius increased with cervical extension effort; cervical spine position (flexed vs neutral) influences recruitment*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Neck | maximum |
| Upper Traps | medium |

## Neck Lateral Flexion
*Sources: Ylinen et al. 2003 — normalized EMG of semispinalis capitis and upper trapezius around 50% MVIC during lateral bending; ipsilateral SCM is primary mover with highest activation during lateral inclination; ipsilateral scalene supports SCM; ipsilateral upper trapezius stabilizes and assists movement; normal synergy hierarchy: SCM > Scalene > Splenius > Upper Trapezius (all ipsilateral); contralateral muscles show minimal activation for stabilization*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Neck | maximum |
| Upper Traps | medium |

## Neck Harness Extension
*Sources: Ylinen et al. 2003 — semispinalis capitis and splenius capitis are primary cervical extensors; upper trapezius is an important contributor to neck extension; neck harness loads the cervical extensors through a greater ROM than manual resistance; position of lower cervical spine influences EMG levels while upper cervical spine position does not; same muscle recruitment pattern as manual neck extension but with progressive external loading capability*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|

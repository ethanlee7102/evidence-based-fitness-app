## BICEPS EXERCISES

---

### 1. Barbell Curl

Standard supinated-grip barbell curl. Classic bilateral biceps exercise.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Supinated grip favors short head; bilateral barbell curl produces very high overall biceps activation |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Both heads heavily loaded; standard grip width activates both heads roughly equally |
| Brachialis | high | Synergist in all elbow flexion; EMG shows meaningful brachialis activation |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Active in elbow flexion but supinated grip reduces brachioradialis contribution vs neutral/pronated |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip and wrist stabilization under load |

**Key evidence:** EMG studies show traditional supinated curl produces highest biceps brachii activation compared to hammer and reverse variants (Marcolin et al. 2018). ACE study ranked cable curl and barbell curl similarly for biceps activation.

---

### 2. EZ-Bar Curl

Semi-supinated grip (~30 degrees pronation) barbell curl. Reduces wrist strain.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Still very high biceps activation; semi-pronated position only slightly reduces biceps vs full supination |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | EZ bar detected higher BB activation than dumbbell curl in some studies |
| Brachialis | high | Semi-pronated position slightly increases brachialis contribution vs full supination |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Higher brachioradialis activation than standard barbell curl due to semi-pronated grip (Marcolin et al. 2018) |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip demand similar to barbell curl |

**Key evidence:** Marcolin et al. (2018) found higher activation of both BB and BR during EZ bar curl compared to dumbbell curl. The semi-pronated position allows biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis to all act near-maximally.

---

### 3. Preacher Curl

Supinated curl performed on angled pad with shoulder in ~45 degrees flexion. Eliminates momentum.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Shoulder flexion biases inner (short) head; EMG confirms preacher curl targets short head preferentially |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Still loaded but shortened position reduces long head contribution relative to short head |
| Brachialis | high | Major synergist; pad eliminates cheating so elbow flexors must do all work |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Active synergist; ACE study showed lower BR activation in preacher curl vs EZ curl |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Oliveira et al. (2009) found preacher curl elicited high activation only for a short range of elbow angle with significant decrease in EMG as elbow flexed. EMG analysis shows specific bias toward inner biceps (short head) due to flexed shoulder position.

---

### 4. Drag Curl

Barbell curl where elbows travel backward (shoulder extension), dragging bar up the torso.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Shoulder extension puts long head in stretched, mechanically favorable position |
| Biceps Short Head | high | Still supinated grip but elbows-back position reduces short head relative contribution |
| Brachialis | medium | Reduced ROM at the elbow compared to standard curl limits brachialis contribution |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Modest contribution; supinated grip reduces brachioradialis role |
| Front Delts | partial | Rear deltoid/posterior shoulder works to drive elbows back; front delts less involved than standard curls |

**Key evidence:** Shoulder extension during the curl biases the long head (which crosses the shoulder joint), consistent with EMG findings on shoulder position effects on biceps activation (Oliveira et al. 2009). Reduced total ROM means less overall load vs standard curl.

---

### 5. Reverse Curl

Pronated (overhand) grip barbell curl.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brachioradialis | maximum | Pronated grip maximizes brachioradialis activation; compensates for mechanically disadvantaged biceps |
| Brachialis | high | Significantly greater brachialis activation during reverse curl vs hammer and traditional curl (Jagessar et al. 2023) |
| Forearm Extensors | high | Pronated grip requires wrist extensors to stabilize against flexion torque; major forearm extensor involvement |
| Biceps Long Head | medium | Biceps activation reduced with pronated grip as tendon wraps around radial tuberosity |
| Biceps Short Head | medium | Both heads reduced equally in pronated position |
| Forearm Flexors | partial | Grip stabilization only |

**Key evidence:** EMG studies confirm brachioradialis excitation was greatest with pronated grip (Barbieri et al. 2023). Brachialis activation significantly greater during reverse curl vs traditional and hammer curls.

---

### 6. Wide-Grip Barbell Curl

Barbell curl with hands wider than shoulder-width, supinated grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Wide grip places elbows closer to torso/behind body, emphasizing short (inner) head |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Still loaded but wide grip shifts relative emphasis to short head |
| Brachialis | high | Standard synergist contribution |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip moderates brachioradialis involvement |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip demand |

**Key evidence:** SuppVersity EMG data shows grip width influences activation patterns of individual heads, with wide grip emphasizing inner (short) head. Total biceps activation remains relatively stable across moderate grip width variations.

---

### 7. Dumbbell Curl

Standard supinated dumbbell curl, unilateral loading.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Supinated grip through full ROM; similar activation pattern to barbell curl |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Both heads heavily loaded; unilateral loading may slightly increase activation via bilateral deficit |
| Brachialis | high | Standard synergist in elbow flexion |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip reduces brachioradialis contribution |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip and supination stabilization |
| Front Delts | partial | Slight anterior deltoid involvement to prevent shoulder extension |

**Key evidence:** Oliveira et al. (2009) found dumbbell biceps curl maintained more consistent activation throughout full ROM including eccentric phase, compared to preacher curl.

---

### 8. Hammer Curl

Neutral (palms-in) grip dumbbell curl.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brachialis | high | Neutral grip shifts emphasis to brachialis; significantly greater activation than traditional curl |
| Brachioradialis | high | Hammer curls showed greatest brachioradialis activation among curl variations |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Long head still active; neutral grip maintains meaningful biceps contribution |
| Biceps Short Head | medium | Short head activation reduced compared to supinated curl; neutral grip is less favorable for short head |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** EMG data shows regular curls produce highest biceps brachii activation while hammer curls elicit greater brachialis and brachioradialis involvement (Marcolin et al. 2018). Neutral grip is mechanically favorable for brachioradialis.

---

### 9. Incline Dumbbell Curl

Supinated dumbbell curl performed on ~45-degree incline bench, shoulders behind torso.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Shoulder extension pre-stretches long head (crosses shoulder joint); EMG confirms long head emphasis |
| Biceps Short Head | high | Still supinated grip but extended shoulder position biases long head |
| Brachialis | high | Standard elbow flexion synergist |
| Brachioradialis | medium | ACE study showed lower BR activation in incline curl vs EZ curl |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Oliveira et al. (2009) found incline dumbbell curl resulted in considerable neuromuscular effort throughout full elbow ROM. EMG analysis shows incline curl provides high, balanced activation favoring outer (long head) biceps. ACE study confirmed significantly less anterior deltoid activation than barbell curl.

---

### 10. Incline Hammer Curl

Neutral grip dumbbell curl performed on ~45-degree incline bench.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Long Head | high | Shoulder extension stretches long head; neutral grip still allows meaningful biceps activation |
| Brachialis | high | Neutral grip emphasizes brachialis; incline reduces momentum |
| Brachioradialis | high | Neutral grip strongly recruits brachioradialis |
| Biceps Short Head | medium | Neutral grip + extended shoulder = less short head activation |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Combines incline position effects (long head stretch, Oliveira et al. 2009) with neutral grip effects (brachialis/brachioradialis emphasis, Marcolin et al. 2018). Load is distributed more evenly across elbow flexors than incline supinated curl.

---

### 11. Concentration Curl

Seated curl with humerus braced against inner thigh, supinated grip, shoulder in ~45 degrees flexion.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | ACE study: highest biceps activation of all exercises tested (97.9% MVIC); shoulder flexion biases short head |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Complete isolation means both heads work hard; humerus bracing prevents momentum |
| Brachialis | high | Pure elbow flexion with no momentum; brachialis must contribute fully |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip reduces brachioradialis role |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** ACE study found concentration curl elicited significantly higher BB activation than all other exercises tested. The mechanism is complete isolation: humerus braced against thigh eliminates upper arm sway, forcing biceps to do all work.

---

### 12. Spider Curl

Prone on incline bench (chest against pad), arms hang straight down, supinated curl. Shoulder in ~90 degrees flexion.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Shoulder flexion + gravity profile provides peak contraction at top; biases short/inner head |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Loaded throughout but flexed shoulder position reduces long head relative to short head |
| Brachialis | high | Gravity resists elbow flexion throughout ROM; strong brachialis demand |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip moderates brachioradialis |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Spider curl provides superior peak contraction intensity at shortened position. Like preacher curl, the flexed shoulder position biases the short head. Eliminates momentum completely.

---

### 13. Zottman Curl

Supinated concentric curl + pronated eccentric lowering, with wrist rotation at top.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Concentric phase is identical to standard supinated curl |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Full supinated curl on the way up |
| Brachioradialis | high | Pronated eccentric phase heavily loads brachioradialis during lowering |
| Brachialis | high | Active in both phases; pronated eccentric increases brachialis demand |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip + wrist rotation demands |
| Pronators & Supinators | medium | Active rotation from supination to pronation and back at top/bottom of each rep |
| Forearm Extensors | medium | Pronated eccentric requires wrist extensor stabilization |

**Key evidence:** The eccentric phase of the Zottman curl enhances forearm muscle usage compared to the concentric phase. Biceps contribute maximally during the supinated concentric, while brachialis, brachioradialis, and forearm muscles brake the pronated descent (Strength & Conditioning Journal, 2014).

---

### 14. Cross-Body Hammer Curl

Dumbbell curled across the body (toward opposite shoulder) with neutral grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brachialis | high | Neutral grip emphasizes brachialis; cross-body path may slightly increase brachialis involvement |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Cross-body movement with slight shoulder adduction places more emphasis on long (outer) head |
| Brachioradialis | high | Neutral grip recruits brachioradialis strongly |
| Biceps Short Head | medium | Neutral grip reduces overall biceps activation vs supinated; cross-body reduces short head relative to standard hammer |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Cross-body hammer curl emphasizes brachialis and long head of biceps slightly more than standard hammer curl. Standard hammer curl is slightly better for brachioradialis/forearm development; cross-body slightly better for biceps peak (long head).

---

### 15. Prone Incline Curl

Lying face-down on ~45-degree incline bench, arms hang straight down, supinated curl. Shoulder in slight flexion.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Prone position eliminates all momentum; similar to spider curl mechanics |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Arms hang with slight shoulder flexion, not full extension; short head slightly favored |
| Brachialis | high | Pure elbow flexion with no body English possible |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip; strict form means no compensatory recruitment |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Similar mechanics to spider curl. The prone position on incline bench eliminates all momentum, forcing strict elbow flexion. Research shows the long head is most prominently targeted when arms are behind the body; here the arms are roughly neutral or slightly forward.

---

### 16. Bayesian Cable Curl

Cable curl performed facing away from machine, arm behind body (shoulder in extension), supinated grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Shoulder extension maximally stretches long head; key advantage of this exercise |
| Biceps Short Head | high | Supinated grip engages both heads, but extended shoulder position biases long head |
| Brachialis | high | Standard elbow flexion synergist |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip; cable provides constant tension |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization against cable traction |

**Key evidence:** EMG study (2025, PMC12550948) found traditional dumbbell curl elicited higher overall BB activation than Bayesian curl. However, the Bayesian curl's shoulder-extended position uniquely stretches the long head. Attarieh et al. (2025) 10-week training study found comparable hypertrophy to preacher curls across proximal, mid, and distal biceps regions.

---

### 17. Cable Hammer Curl

Neutral grip curl using rope attachment on low cable.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brachialis | high | Neutral grip emphasizes brachialis, same as dumbbell hammer curl |
| Brachioradialis | high | Neutral grip maximizes brachioradialis; cable provides constant tension throughout ROM |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Still meaningful biceps activation; constant cable tension may increase time under tension |
| Biceps Short Head | medium | Neutral grip reduces short head contribution |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Rope grip demand |

**Key evidence:** Same grip mechanics as dumbbell hammer curl but with constant cable tension. Cable eliminates the dead spot at top/bottom of dumbbell version. Neutral grip principles well-established in EMG literature (Barbieri et al. 2023).

---

### 18. Overhead Cable Curl

Curl performed from high pulley position with arms abducted to ~shoulder height, supinated grip (front double biceps pose).

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Shoulder abduction + flexion biases short head; peak contraction at shortened position |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Both heads work but short head is favored in this arm position |
| Brachialis | medium | Shorter effective ROM at elbow; less brachialis demand than standard curls |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization |
| Front Delts | partial | Isometric shoulder stabilization |

**Key evidence:** High pulley position provides intense peak contraction that is virtually unrivaled. Constant cable tension maintains biceps engagement even at full contraction. The shortened position primarily challenges the short head.

---

### 19. Single-Arm Cable Curl

Unilateral supinated cable curl from low pulley.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Same mechanics as bilateral cable curl; supinated grip |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Constant cable tension loads both heads throughout ROM |
| Brachialis | high | Standard elbow flexion synergist |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip stabilization; unilateral = more core/stabilizer demand |

**Key evidence:** Unilateral exercises may elicit slightly greater muscle activation via bilateral deficit phenomenon (JSCR research). Same fundamental mechanics as standard cable curl with constant tension advantage. Activation profile matches calibration example (Cable Curl).

---

### 20. Machine Preacher Curl

Preacher curl performed on machine (cam-based or plate-loaded), supinated grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Flexed shoulder position biases short head, same as free weight preacher |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Loaded but shortened position reduces long head contribution |
| Brachialis | high | Machine provides consistent resistance throughout ROM, unlike free weight version |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip |
| Forearm Flexors | partial | Machine handles reduce grip demand vs free weight |

**Key evidence:** Machine provides more consistent tension throughout full ROM compared to free weight preacher curl, which has high activation only at beginning of concentric phase (Oliveira et al. 2009). Both versions produce comparable hypertrophy results. Machine version eliminates the dead spot at top of free weight preacher.

---

### 21. Machine Bicep Curl

Seated machine curl (e.g., plate-loaded or selectorized), supinated grip on handles, arms on pad.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Fixed path, pad-supported arms; similar shoulder position to preacher curl |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Both heads work but machine arm pad position typically favors short head |
| Brachialis | high | Consistent resistance curve via cam/lever system |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Supinated grip |
| Forearm Flexors | partial | Machine handles reduce grip demand |

**Key evidence:** Selectorized machines provide more stability and may result in greater activation of primary movers compared to cable-based setups. Fixed movement path eliminates stabilizer demand, focusing neural drive on biceps.

## TRICEPS EXERCISES

---

### 22. Skull Crusher

Lying on bench, lowering barbell/EZ bar toward forehead or behind head, elbows pointed at ceiling.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Shoulder flexion stretches long head across shoulder joint; behind-head trajectory maximizes this |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All three heads contribute to elbow extension; lateral and medial are strong synergists |
| Front Delts | partial | Isometric shoulder stabilization to maintain arm position |

**Key evidence:** ACE study measured skull crushers at ~62% MVIC overall. EMG studies show skull crushers allow all three heads to engage maximally when shoulders are held steady. Lowering behind head (vs to forehead) increases long head stretch and activation.

---

### 23. JM Press

Hybrid between close-grip bench press and skull crusher. Bar lowered to chin/throat area with elbows flaring slightly.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Pressing component at horizontal shoulder angle strongly activates lateral and medial heads |
| Triceps Long Head | high | Elbow flexion component engages long head, but pressing mechanics reduce full stretch vs skull crusher |
| Front Delts | medium | Pressing component involves anterior deltoid more than pure extensions |
| Mid Chest | partial | Some pectoral involvement due to pressing mechanics, though minimal with proper form |

**Key evidence:** The JM Press is a hybrid — the pressing component favors lateral/medial heads (activated at 75-100 degree shoulder angles), while the skull crusher component partially stretches the long head. Allows heavier loading than pure extensions.

---

### 24. Floor Skull Crusher

Skull crusher performed lying on the floor instead of a bench. Reduced ROM.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | Floor limits ROM, reducing the stretch phase; still primary movers for elbow extension |
| Triceps Long Head | high | Reduced ability to lower weight behind head limits long head stretch vs bench version |
| Front Delts | partial | Isometric stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Floor removes the deep stretch portion of the movement. Research indicates ROM is a key variable in muscle activation; floor skull crushers sacrifice long head stretch. Still effective for all three heads but slightly reduced overall triceps demand vs bench version due to shortened ROM.

---

### 25. Dumbbell Kickback

Bent-over single-arm triceps extension, arm parallel to floor, extending elbow to lockout.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Shoulder extension + full elbow extension; ACE study ranked kickbacks among top 3 exercises |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute to elbow extension at lockout |

**Key evidence:** ACE study found no significant difference between triangle push-ups, kickbacks, and dips for long head or lateral head activation. Incline dumbbell kickback (face-down on 30-degree bench) is ~20% more effective than standard kickback for long head. Peak activation occurs at full lockout where gravity resistance is maximal.

---

### 26. Overhead Tricep Extension

Standing or seated, dumbbell or barbell held overhead, lowered behind head by flexing elbows.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Overhead position maximally stretches long head across shoulder joint |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute; lateral/medial less emphasized than long head in overhead position |

**Key evidence:** Maeo et al. (2022) found overhead extensions produced 1.5x greater long head hypertrophy than pushdowns (+28.5% vs +19.6%) over 12 weeks, AND 1.4x greater lateral+medial head growth (+14.6% vs +10.5%). ACE study measured overhead extension at ~76% MVIC.

---

### 27. Tate Press

Lying on bench, dumbbells held above chest with elbows flared, lowering weights inward to chest by flexing elbows.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Horizontal shoulder angle + flared elbows strongly bias lateral and medial heads |
| Triceps Long Head | medium | Shoulders at ~90 degrees; medial head is primary mover, long head contributes less than overhead exercises |
| Mid Chest | partial | Some pectoral involvement at bottom of movement due to arm position |

**Key evidence:** EMG data shows medial head is most involved in the Tate Press. The unique elbow angle particularly engages the lateral head. At 90-degree shoulder elevation, medial head shows significantly higher force than long head. Activation sequence: medial > lateral > long.

---

### 28. Single-Arm Dumbbell Overhead Extension

Single-arm version of overhead extension, dumbbell lowered behind head.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Same overhead mechanics as bilateral version; long head maximally stretched |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute to elbow extension |

**Key evidence:** Same principles as bilateral overhead extension (Maeo et al. 2022). Unilateral version allows greater ROM and individual arm focus. Overhead position stretches long head as it crosses both shoulder and elbow joints.

---

### 29. Tricep Pushdown

Cable pushdown with straight bar or V-bar, overhand (pronated) grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Arms at sides (neutral shoulder); lateral head is primary mover with overhand grip |
| Triceps Long Head | medium | Long head not stretched at shoulder (arms at sides); contributes but is not primary |

**Key evidence:** ACE study measured bar pushdown at ~67% MVIC. Straight bar primarily targets lateral head contributing to "horseshoe" shape. Overhead extensions produce 40% more triceps growth than pushdowns (Maeo et al. 2022), primarily due to greater long head activation.

---

### 30. Single-Arm Cable Pushdown

Unilateral cable pushdown with handle, overhand or neutral grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Same mechanics as bilateral pushdown; lateral/medial heads are primary |
| Triceps Long Head | medium | Arm at side; long head not maximally stretched |

**Key evidence:** Same activation profile as bilateral pushdown. Unilateral training may produce slightly greater activation via bilateral deficit, plus allows correction of asymmetries.

---

### 31. Cable Overhead Extension

Cable extension performed facing away from machine, arms overhead, extending elbows forward/upward.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Overhead position maximally stretches long head; cable provides constant tension through full ROM |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute to elbow extension; constant cable tension may increase total activation |

**Key evidence:** Same overhead position advantage as dumbbell overhead extension (Maeo et al. 2022). Cable version adds constant tension throughout ROM, eliminating dead spots. Rope attachment allows slight outward rotation at extension for enhanced peak contraction.

---

### 32. Tricep Pushdown (Rope)

Cable pushdown using rope attachment, allowing hand separation at bottom.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Neutral grip; rope allows splitting at bottom for peak contraction |
| Triceps Long Head | medium | Arms at sides; long head contributes but not primary. Rope may slightly increase long head activation vs bar due to ROM |

**Key evidence:** ACE study measured rope pushdown at ~74% MVIC (higher than bar pushdown at ~67%). EMG shows significant difference between long and lateral head activation during rope pushdowns. Rope allows greater ROM and peak contraction by splitting ends past body plane.

---

### 33. Tricep Pushdown (Reverse Grip)

Cable pushdown with supinated (underhand) grip on straight bar.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute; supinated grip shifts emphasis toward medial head specifically |
| Triceps Long Head | medium | Arm at side; similar to other pushdown variants for long head |

**Key evidence:** European Journal of Applied Physiology study found reverse-grip pushdown elicited significantly higher medial head activation vs overhand pushdown. Medial head ~60% MVC, lateral ~45%, long ~30%. Forward/overhand grip favors lateral head; reverse grip favors medial head.

---

### 34. Cable Kickback

Triceps kickback performed on cable machine, bent over, extending arm behind body.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Shoulder extension + full elbow extension; same mechanics as dumbbell kickback with constant cable tension |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute at lockout |

**Key evidence:** Cable version provides constant tension vs dumbbell which only has peak loading at lockout. ACE study ranked kickbacks among top 3 triceps exercises. Cable tension maintains load throughout entire ROM, potentially increasing overall activation vs dumbbell version.

---

### 35. Machine Tricep Extension

Seated machine (plate-loaded or selectorized), arms on pad, extending elbows against resistance. Typically overhead or elevated arm position.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Most machine tricep extensions position arms overhead/in front, stretching long head |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | Fixed movement path; all heads contribute |

**Key evidence:** Machine provides consistent resistance curve throughout ROM. Fixed path eliminates stabilizer demand, directing neural drive to triceps. Overhead arm position on most machines stretches long head similar to overhead cable extension.

---

### 36. Assisted Dip Machine

Dip performed on machine with counterweight assistance, reducing bodyweight load.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | Same dip mechanics but reduced load; all three heads heavily involved |
| Triceps Long Head | high | Shoulder extension through dip ROM stretches long head |
| Lower Chest | medium | Pectoral involvement in dip pattern; chest activation increases with forward lean |
| Front Delts | medium | Anterior deltoid contributes to pressing pattern |

**Key evidence:** Bench/bar/ring dip EMG study (PMC9603242) showed high triceps activation across all dip variants. ACE study ranked dips among top 3 triceps exercises. Assisted version reduces load while maintaining same movement pattern and muscle recruitment pattern.

---

### 37. Tricep Dip

Bodyweight dip on parallel bars with upright torso, elbows close to body.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Compound movement; upright torso emphasizes triceps over chest |
| Triceps Long Head | high | Shoulder extension through ROM stretches long head; dips ranked among top 3 in ACE study |
| Lower Chest | medium | Pectoral involvement increases with depth and forward lean |
| Front Delts | medium | Anterior deltoid contributes to pressing movement |

**Key evidence:** ACE study found no significant difference between triangle push-ups, kickbacks, and dips for combined long + lateral head EMG. Mean peak triceps activation: bench dip 0.83mV, bar dip 1.04mV, ring dip 1.05mV (PMC9603242). Weighted dips elicit high activation in all three heads.

---

### 38. Bench Dip

Dip performed between two benches (hands on bench behind, feet on bench in front).

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | maximum | Primary mover; bench dip heavily isolates triceps due to body position |
| Triceps Long Head | high | Shoulder extension ROM is present but more limited than parallel bar dips |
| Front Delts | medium | Anterior deltoid involvement; greater shoulder extension demand than parallel dips |
| Lower Chest | partial | Less chest involvement than parallel bar dips due to body position |

**Key evidence:** EMG study showed bench dip peak triceps activation of 0.83mV vs bar dip 1.04mV (PMC9603242). ACE study ranked bench dips somewhat lower than parallel bar dips. Greater shoulder stress due to hands-behind positioning; requires adequate shoulder mobility.

---

### 39. Bodyweight Tricep Extension

Standing or kneeling, hands on elevated surface (bar, bench), lowering head forward by bending elbows, then pressing back. Essentially a bodyweight skull crusher.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Long Head | maximum | Overhead/elevated arm position stretches long head, similar to skull crusher mechanics |
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | All heads contribute to elbow extension; compound bodyweight movement |
| Front Delts | partial | Shoulder stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Mechanics mirror skull crusher / overhead extension pattern. Arms move overhead relative to torso, stretching the long head across the shoulder joint. Bodyweight loading means resistance is progressive with body angle — more horizontal = harder.

---

### 40. Band Pushdown

Tricep pushdown using resistance band anchored overhead.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triceps Lateral & Medial | high | Same pushdown mechanics as cable; band provides ascending resistance (hardest at lockout) |
| Triceps Long Head | medium | Arm at side; same position as cable pushdown |

**Key evidence:** Band provides variable resistance (ascending strength curve) vs cable's constant tension. Peak resistance at lockout where triceps are strongest. Same fundamental movement pattern and muscle recruitment as cable pushdown, but resistance profile differs. Lighter at stretch, heavier at contraction.

## FOREARM EXERCISES

---

### 41. Behind-the-Back Wrist Curl

Standing, holding barbell behind body with supinated grip, flexing wrists.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Primary wrist flexors (FCR, FCU) produce the curling force; standing position increases load vs seated |

**Key evidence:** Behind-the-back position forces forearm flexors to work harder against load due to wrists/hands being behind center of gravity. Stretches muscles under tension more effectively than seated version, enhancing hypertrophy signaling in deep flexor tissues. Greater effective load than bench wrist curl.

---

### 42. Plate Pinch Hold

Holding weight plates together between thumb and fingers in isometric pinch grip.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Finger flexors (FDP, FDS) and thumb flexors contract isometrically to maintain pinch |
| Forearm Extensors | medium | Wrist extensors co-contract for wrist stabilization during pinch |

**Key evidence:** Pinch grip training targets the finger flexors and thumb adductor/flexor muscles specifically. EMG research on grip force shows forearm muscle activity amplitude increases proportionally with grip force level. Isometric hold trains grip endurance and crush strength.

---

### 43. Towel Pull-Up

Pull-up performed gripping a towel draped over the bar instead of the bar itself.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Forced towel squeezing to support bodyweight dramatically increases forearm flexor demand |
| Upper Lats | high | Standard pull-up primary mover |
| Biceps Short Head | high | Grip position varies; supinated emphasis |
| Biceps Long Head | high | Standard elbow flexion contribution |
| Brachialis | medium | Elbow flexion synergist |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Neutral/variable grip on towel |
| Lower Lats | medium | Pull-up synergist |
| Mid Traps | medium | Scapular retraction |
| Rear Delts | medium | Shoulder extension component |

**Key evidence:** EMG study (PMC5548150) found towel pull-up variations facilitate similar effects on major pulling muscles as standard pull-ups. The towel grip heavily trains "gripping" and "crushing" strength due to forced squeezing. This is primarily a back exercise with forearm as a major limiting factor.

---

### 44. Fat Grip Barbell Hold

Holding a barbell with fat grips (or thick bar) in static hold at lockout position.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Thicker diameter dramatically increases finger flexor and grip demand |
| Forearm Extensors | medium | Wrist stabilization co-contraction increases with thicker grip |
| Upper Traps | partial | Isometric support of load |

**Key evidence:** Research found fat grips resulted in ~30% increase in forearm activation. EMG muscle activity was significantly increased in forearm and shoulder muscles but decreased in upper arm muscles with fat grips (PubMed 30694963). The larger diameter forces fingers to work in a mechanically disadvantaged, more open position.

---

### 45. Wrist Roller

Rolling a weighted cord up/down using wrist flexion/extension rotation while arms are extended.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Wrist flexion phase is primary; concentric forearm flexor work to wind cord up |
| Forearm Extensors | high | Wrist extension phase (rolling up with extension, or controlled lowering with flexion) heavily loads extensors |
| Front Delts | partial | Isometric shoulder flexion to hold arms extended (if held in front of body) |

**Key evidence:** EMG research shows wrist flexion causes ~2x higher amplitude in flexors than wrist extension does in extensors. Wrist roller uniquely trains both flexors and extensors in alternating fashion. Both concentric rolling-up and eccentric lowering provide substantial forearm stimulus.

---

### 46. Pronation/Supination

Rotating forearm from palm-down to palm-up (and reverse) against resistance, typically with dumbbell or hammer-grip implement.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pronators & Supinators | maximum | Pronator teres and pronator quadratus are primary pronators; supinator muscle is primary supinator |
| Forearm Flexors | partial | FCR shows some activity during pronation |
| Forearm Extensors | partial | ECU shows some activity during pronation |
| Biceps Short Head | partial | Biceps brachii is a secondary supinator at higher torques |

**Key evidence:** EMG study confirmed pronator teres and pronator quadratus show major contributions to pronation torque. Supinator dominates at low torques; biceps brachii increasingly contributes at higher supination torques. At low-to-moderate loads, this is a true isolation exercise for pronators and supinators.

---

### 47. Finger Curl

Seated, barbell resting on fingertips (with forearms on thighs or bench), curling fingers to close hand.

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Finger flexors (FDP, FDS) are the primary movers; isolated finger flexion with minimal wrist movement |

**Key evidence:** Finger curls isolate the finger flexor muscles (flexor digitorum profundus and superficialis) which run along the underside of the forearm. More targeted than wrist curls which involve both finger and wrist flexors. True isolation movement for crushing grip strength.

---

### 48. Cable Curl
*Sources: Marcolin et al. 2018 (PMC6047503), Coratella et al. 2023 (PMC10054060) — biceps both heads are prime movers in supinated cable curls. Constant tension from cable maintains load at top of movement unlike barbell curl.*

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biceps Short Head | maximum | Supinated grip, constant cable tension |
| Biceps Long Head | maximum | Both heads equally loaded with supinated grip |
| Brachialis | high | Synergist in all elbow flexion |
| Brachioradialis | medium | Active in elbow flexion; reduced vs neutral grip |
| Forearm Flexors | medium | Grip and wrist stabilization |
| Front Delts | partial | Minimal shoulder stabilization |

**Key evidence:** Cable provides constant tension through the full ROM unlike free weights which lose tension at the top. Standard straight-bar cable curl; for constant-tension variants see Bayesian Cable Curl (#16).

---

### 49. Wrist Curl
*Sources: Isolation exercise — Forearm Flexors are the only meaningful mover in a pure wrist flexion exercise. Seated with forearms on thighs or bench.*

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Flexors | maximum | Pure wrist flexion; FDP, FDS, FCR, FCU all active |

**Key evidence:** Single-muscle isolation — no meaningful synergists. Same recruitment pattern as Behind-the-Back Wrist Curl (#41) and Finger Curl (#47) but targeting wrist flexion rather than grip.

---

### 50. Reverse Wrist Curl
*Sources: Isolation exercise — Forearm Extensors are the only meaningful mover in a pure wrist extension exercise.*

| Muscle | Activation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm Extensors | maximum | Pure wrist extension; ECR longus/brevis, ECU all active |

**Key evidence:** Single-muscle isolation — no meaningful synergists. Antagonist to Wrist Curl (#49). Often underemphasized relative to flexor training.

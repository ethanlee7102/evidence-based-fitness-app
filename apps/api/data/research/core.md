# Core Exercises - EMG-Backed Muscle Activation Mappings

Status: 36 exercises (3 previously researched, 33 new)

Sources:
- ACE-sponsored study (San Diego State University, Peter Francis Ph.D.) — EMG comparison of 13 abdominal exercises, MVIC-normalized, bicycle crunch #1 rectus abdominis, captain's chair #1 obliques
- Escamilla et al. 2006 (Physical Therapy 86(5):656) — EMG of traditional and nontraditional ab exercises including Power Wheel rollout (URA 76%, LRA 81% MVIC), pike, knee-up, hanging knee-up, reverse crunch inclined
- Escamilla et al. 2010 (JOSPT 40(5):265) — Core muscle activation during Swiss ball and traditional exercises; rollout URA 63%, LRA 53%, EO 46%; pike EO 84%
- Youdas et al. 2019 (JOSPT) — Magnitudes of muscle activation during prone planking with and without fitness ball
- Czaprowski et al. 2022 (PMC9505236) — Surface EMG of RA and EO during isometric and dynamic exercises
- Konrad 2005 (JOSPT) — EMG analysis of core rehabilitation exercises in side-bridge position
- Barrios et al. 2020 (PubMed 32369764) — Surface EMG of internal and external obliques during isometric lateral trunk tasks, side plank IO 107% MVIC, EO 72% MVIC
- Saeterbakken et al. 2019 — Comparison of core stability exercises; bird dog highest TrA activation
- Huxel Bliven & Anderson 2013 — Core stability and its relationship to upper/lower extremity function
- Contreras & Schoenfeld 2011 — Analysis of single vs multi-joint exercises for core training
- McGill 2010 — Core training: evidence translating to better performance and injury prevention
- Lehman et al. 2005 — Variations in muscle activation during plank exercises
- Boeckh-Behrens & Buskies 2000 — EMG of ab exercises in trained males (SuppVersity EMG Series)
- Sternlicht & Rugg 2003 (PubMed 12580657) — EMG comparison of crunch variations with external loads
- Vera-Garcia et al. 2007 (Physical Therapy) — Ab curl-up effectiveness on unstable vs stable surfaces
- Comfort et al. 2011 — Loaded carry mechanics and EMG of stabilizer muscles
- Hibbs et al. 2008 — Designing core stability training programs using EMG evidence

## UPPER ABS / FLEXION

### Crunch
*Sources: ACE study — traditional crunch used as baseline (100% MVIC reference); Escamilla et al. 2006 — crunch URA ~40% MVIC, LRA ~35%, EO ~25%; Boeckh-Behrens & Buskies 2000 — crunch ranks mid-tier for rectus abdominis among dynamic exercises; Sternlicht & Rugg 2003 — crunch produces moderate RA activation with minimal hip flexor involvement compared to sit-ups*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Upper Abs | maximum |
| Lower Abs | medium |
| Obliques | partial |

### Bicycle Crunch
*Sources: ACE study — #1 for rectus abdominis (248% of crunch baseline), #2 for obliques (290% of crunch); Escamilla et al. 2006 — high RA and EO activation due to combined trunk flexion + rotation + hip flexion; the rotational component significantly increases oblique activation vs standard crunch*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Upper Abs | maximum |
| Obliques | maximum |
| Lower Abs | high |
| Rectus Femoris | partial |

### Decline Sit-Up
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — bent-knee sit-up with incline increases both RA and hip flexor activation vs flat; EMG studies show decline increases gravitational resistance and ROM, yielding higher RA EMG than flat sit-ups; hip flexor (iliopsoas, rectus femoris) involvement increases significantly at decline angles*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Upper Abs | maximum |
| Lower Abs | high |
| Obliques | medium |
| Rectus Femoris | medium |
| Erector Spinae | partial |

### Cable Crunch
*Sources: Sternlicht & Rugg 2003 — adding external load to crunch movement significantly increases URA and LRA activation; kneeling cable crunch allows progressive overload while maintaining trunk-flexion-dominant pattern; EMG shows high RA activation with minimal hip flexor involvement due to fixed hip position*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Upper Abs | maximum |
| Lower Abs | high |
| Obliques | medium |
| Erector Spinae | partial |

### Sit-Up
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — bent-knee sit-up shows moderate URA (~38% MVIC), LRA (~32%), high rectus femoris activity; ACE study — sit-up recruits hip flexors significantly more than crunch; sit-up involves full trunk flexion + hip flexion, shifting load to iliopsoas in second half of ROM*

| Muscle Group | Activation |

| Upper Abs | maximum |
| Lower Abs | medium |
| Obliques | partial |
| Rectus Femoris | medium |
| Erector Spinae | partial |

### V-Up
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — supine V-up shows similar URA and LRA to crunch but significantly higher internal oblique activation; V-up involves simultaneous trunk flexion and hip flexion, requiring both upper and lower RA plus hip flexors; rectus femoris EMG significantly higher than crunch*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Upper Abs | maximum |
| Lower Abs | high |
| Obliques | medium |
| Rectus Femoris | medium |

### Weighted Crunch
*Sources: Sternlicht & Rugg 2003 — external load during crunch significantly increases RA EMG amplitude proportionally to load; Boeckh-Behrens & Buskies 2000 — weighted trunk flexion among highest rectus abdominis activators; adding load does not alter oblique recruitment pattern substantially*

### Machine Crunch
*Sources: Boeckh-Behrens & Buskies 2000 — machine crunch allows progressive overload with guided ROM, producing high RA activation; ACE study — machine-guided exercises generally show lower RA than free-motion equivalents due to reduced stabilizer demands; fixed path reduces oblique contribution vs free crunches; hip flexor involvement minimal due to seated/fixed position*

| Muscle Group | Activation |

| Obliques | partial |

### GHD Sit-Up
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — sit-ups with hip extension ROM produce higher RA EMG than flat sit-ups due to eccentric lengthening through extended ROM; GHD sit-up involves full spinal extension to flexion + hip flexion, producing very high RA activation but also significant hip flexor (iliopsoas, rectus femoris) demand; erector spinae active eccentrically during descent*

## LOWER ABS / HIP FLEXION

### Hanging Leg Raise
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — hanging knee-up with straps among highest LRA activators (~45% MVIC); straight-leg version increases lever arm and RA demand further; ACE study — captain's chair (similar mechanics) ranked #2 for RA, #1 for obliques; hip flexors heavily recruited but RA demand is high due to posterior pelvic tilt requirement against gravity*

|---|---|
| Lower Abs | maximum |
| Upper Abs | high |
| Obliques | high |
| Rectus Femoris | high |
| Front Delts | partial |
| Forearm Flexors | partial |

### Toes-to-Bar
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — full ROM hanging leg exercises produce highest LRA activation when legs reach bar height; posterior pelvic tilt at top demands extreme lower RA contraction; Contreras & Schoenfeld 2011 — hanging exercises with full pelvic rotation maximize lower RA vs hip flexors; grip and lat isometric demand present*

### Lying Leg Raise
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — supine double straight leg raise shows moderate LRA activation (~35% MVIC); hip flexors are primary movers early in ROM; RA activation increases when posterior pelvic tilt is maintained; without pelvic tilt, exercise is predominantly hip flexor dominant; EO contributes as stabilizer*

| Muscle Group | Activation |

### Reverse Crunch
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — reverse crunch inclined 30° among highest LRA activators; ACE study — reverse crunch ranked #7 for RA but #3 for obliques; posterior pelvic tilt lifts hips, preferentially targeting lower RA; hip flexor involvement lower than leg raises due to shorter lever arm and pelvic-curl-dominant pattern*

### Flutter Kick
*Sources: EMG analysis shows continuous isometric lower RA demand to maintain pelvis position while alternating hip flexion; hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris) are primary movers of the kicking motion; lower RA acts as stabilizer preventing anterior pelvic tilt; obliques contribute to trunk stabilization; moderate overall RA activation compared to crunches*

## OBLIQUES / ROTATION

### Russian Twist
*Sources: Boeckh-Behrens & Buskies 2000 — trunk rotation exercises rank high for oblique EMG; the seated position with trunk lean engages upper and lower RA isometrically while rotation targets obliques; EMG shows high EO and IO activation during dynamic rotation; rectus abdominis moderately active as trunk stabilizer*

| Lower Abs | medium |
| Erector Spinae | partial |

### Wood Chop
*Sources: Escamilla et al. 2006 — rotational trunk exercises produce high oblique EMG; wood chop involves combined rotation + flexion, activating both IO and EO through full ROM; anterior deltoid active during arm movement; RA moderately active as trunk flexor/stabilizer; erector spinae co-contracts for stability*

### Oblique Crunch

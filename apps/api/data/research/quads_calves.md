## QUAD EXERCISES

### 1. Barbell Back Squat

The gold standard compound movement. EMG studies (Yavuz et al. 2015, Contreras et al. 2015) show high vasti activation throughout, with peak quadriceps activity at 80-90 degrees of knee flexion. Rectus femoris is less active than vasti due to active insufficiency at depth (hip flexed = RF shortened at hip). Gluteus maximus activation increases with depth. Erector spinae highly active for spinal stabilization under axial load.

|---|---|

| Gluteus Maximus | high |
| Rectus Femoris | medium |
| Hip Extensors | high |
| Knee Flexors | medium |
| Erector Spinae | high |
| Upper Abs | medium |
| Lower Abs | medium |
| Gluteus Medius & Minimus | partial |
| Gastrocnemius | partial |
| Soleus | partial |

### 2. Front Squat

EMG studies (Gullett et al. 2009, Yavuz et al. 2015) show the highest RF, VL, and VM EMG activities of all squat variations tested. Significantly greater vastus medialis activation vs back squat. More upright torso reduces erector spinae demand relative to back squat. Front-loaded position increases core demands. RF more active than in back squat due to greater knee flexion moment relative to hip moment.

### 3. Box Squat

Concentric-dominant due to pause on box eliminating stretch-shortening cycle. The pause at bottom reduces elastic energy from the vasti, requiring more concentric recruitment from hip extensors (glutes/hamstrings) to initiate ascent. Quad activation comparable to back squat but glute/hamstring emphasis is relatively greater.

### 4. Pause Squat

Similar to back squat but the isometric pause at bottom eliminates the stretch-shortening cycle, increasing concentric demand on the vasti. EMG data shows vasti and erector spinae activation is very high at the bottom position during the isometric hold. Glutes receive high stretch at bottom but EMG activation during the pause is moderate; they contribute heavily through elastic recoil upon ascent.

### 10. Leg Press

Systematic review (Rossi et al. 2020, PMC7369968): VL and VM elicit greatest activation, followed by RF. High foot placement increases glute max and hamstring activation. Low foot placement emphasizes quadriceps. Minimal erector spinae activation (back supported). No balance/stabilization demands.

|---|---|

### 11. Leg Extension

Open-chain knee extension in isolation. All four quadriceps heads highly active (Signorile et al. 2014). Rectus femoris activation is high because hip is fixed (no active insufficiency). VMO and VL show highest peak EMG at ~90 degrees knee flexion. No hip extensor or stabilizer demand. Truly isolates knee extensors.

| Muscle | Activation |
|---|---|
| Vastus Group | maximum |
| Rectus Femoris | maximum |

### 12. Hack Squat

Yavuz et al. (2015): Erector spinae and semitendinosus EMG significantly lower than all other squat variations (back supported, guided path). Quadriceps emphasized due to fixed back angle pushing knees forward. Reduced hamstring activation vs free-weight squats. Very quad-dominant machine movement.

| Muscle | Activation |
|---|---|
| Vastus Group | maximum |
| Rectus Femoris | high |
| Gluteus Maximus | medium |
| Hip Extensors | medium |
| Knee Flexors | partial |

### 13. Pendulum Squat

Arc motion creates progressive resistance curve that intensifies at the bottom. The curved path keeps quads under tension longer than hack squat's linear path. Allows deeper ROM than hack squat. Back is supported so minimal erector spinae demand. Greater glute/hamstring contribution at bottom of arc than hack squat due to deeper hip flexion.

| Muscle | Activation |
|---|---|
| Vastus Group | maximum |
| Rectus Femoris | high |
| Gluteus Maximus | high |
| Hip Extensors | medium |
| Knee Flexors | partial |

### 14. Smith Machine Squat

Schwanbeck et al. (2009): Free-weight squats elicited 43% higher average overall EMG than Smith machine. VM was 49% higher in free-weight. However, feet-forward position on Smith machine can increase VL activation. No significant difference in glute max activation between Smith machine and free-weight squats. Fixed path removes stabilizer demand.

### 15. Belt Squat

Evans et al. (2019): No significant difference in VM, VL, RF, or glute max activation vs back squat. Key difference: eliminates axial spinal loading entirely. Higher hip abductor-to-adductor ratio than barbell squat. No erector spinae demand (no bar on back). Load is suspended from hips.

### 16. V-Squat Machine

### 17. Sissy Squat

Unique biomechanics: hips stay extended while knees flex deeply. This places a maximal stretch on rectus femoris (lengthened at both joints). One of the few squat variations that truly challenges the RF. Functions similarly to a bodyweight leg extension. Minimal posterior chain involvement. Highly quad-dominant.

### 23. Cyclist Squat

## CALF EXERCISES

### 28. Standing Calf Raise

Riemann et al. (2012), Signorile et al. (2002): Knee-extended position maximizes gastrocnemius length and activation. Lateral and medial gastrocnemius operate at longer muscle lengths in standing vs seated. Soleus contributes but is not preferentially targeted. Parallel foot position activates both heads of gastrocnemius maximally.

| Muscle | Activation |
|---|---|
| Gastrocnemius | maximum |
| Soleus | medium |
| Tibialis Anterior | partial |

### 29. Seated Calf Raise

Riemann et al. (2012): Bent knee position (typically 90 degrees) shortens and weakens the biarticular gastrocnemius, shifting demand to the soleus. Soleus volume changes similar between standing and seated conditions (2.1% vs 2.9%), while gastrocnemius hypertrophy is dramatically lower seated (0.6-1.7% vs 9.2-12.4% standing). This confirms the soleus-dominant, gastrocnemius-diminished activation pattern.

| Muscle | Activation |
|---|---|
| Soleus | maximum |
| Gastrocnemius | medium |

### 30. Donkey Calf Raise

Boeckh-Behrens & Buskies (2000): Highest overall calf EMG activity of all calf variations tested. Knee extended (like standing raise) so gastrocnemius fully active. Hip flexion with straight knees may place gastrocnemius at an even longer operating length than standing, explaining the slight EMG advantage. The original Suppversity EMG ranking places this at #1 for overall calf activation.

| Muscle | Activation |
|---|---|
| Gastrocnemius | maximum |
| Soleus | medium |
| Tibialis Anterior | partial |

### 31. Smith Machine Calf Raise

## Summary of Key EMG Principles Applied

### Quadriceps Distinctions
- **Vastus Group** (VL + VM + VI): Primary knee extensors. Active in all squat/press/extension movements. Peak EMG at 80-90 degrees knee flexion.
- **Rectus Femoris**: Biarticular (hip + knee). Most active when hip is NOT deeply flexed (leg extension, sissy squat, Spanish squat). Experiences active insufficiency in deep squats where it's shortened at the hip. Front squat shows higher RF activation than back squat due to greater knee-to-hip moment ratio.

### Calf Distinctions
- **Gastrocnemius**: Biarticular (knee + ankle). Maximally active with knee extended (standing/donkey calf raise). Shortened and weakened with knee bent.
- **Soleus**: Monoarticular (ankle only). Active regardless of knee angle. Preferentially targeted with bent knee (seated calf raise) since gastrocnemius contribution is reduced.

### Machine vs Free Weight
- Machine-guided exercises (hack squat, leg press, Smith machine) reduce stabilizer demand (erector spinae, core, gluteus medius)
- Free-weight squats elicit ~43% higher overall EMG than Smith machine (Schwanbeck et al. 2009)
- Belt squat matches quad/glute activation of back squat while eliminating spinal loading

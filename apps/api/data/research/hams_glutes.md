# Hamstring, Glute & Adductor Exercise Muscle Activation Research (EMG-Backed)

Status: 39 exercises researched. All mappings verified against EMG studies from PMC, JSCR, PLOS One, ACE, and published peer-reviewed research.

Activation levels: maximum (primary mover), high (major synergist), medium (contributes but not primary), partial (stabilizer/minimal)

Muscle groups match exactly from the 36-group taxonomy.

## HAMSTRING EXERCISES (16)

### 1. Romanian Deadlift
*Sources: PMC7046193 (Martins-Costa et al. 2020 — systematic review of deadlift EMG), PMC8835508 (Hegedus et al. 2022 — RDL vs stiff-leg DL), McAllister et al. 2014 (JSCR — hamstring exercise comparison). BF long head + ST very high EMG (~70-115% MVIC). Erector spinae lower than BF/ST. Glute max moderate (hip extension contributor but not peak mover). Adductors assist hip extension.*

### 8. Lying Leg Curl
*Sources: McAllister et al. 2014 (JSCR), PubMed 35586943 (seated vs lying curl comparison), PMC4492645 (hamstring exercise classification). Prone position = hips extended = hamstrings shortened at hip. Supine/prone curls preferentially target BF over ST. Knee flexors are primary. Hip extensors minimal (hips already extended). Gastrocnemius assists knee flexion.*

| Muscle Group | Activation |
|---|---|
| Knee Flexors | maximum |
| Hip Extensors | medium |
| Gastrocnemius | partial |

### 9. Seated Leg Curl
*Sources: PubMed 35586943 (seated vs lying curl EMG + hypertrophy comparison), JSCR 2018. Seated position = hips flexed = hamstrings lengthened at hip. 55% more hypertrophy than lying curl after 12 weeks. More even activation across all hamstring heads (BF long, SM, ST). Short head BF similar between variations.*

## Key EMG Research Sources

1. **McAllister et al. 2014** (JSCR) — Hamstring exercise comparison: leg curl, good morning, glute-ham raise, RDL. ST > BF in RDL and GHR.
2. **Contreras et al. 2015** (PubMed 26214739) — Hip thrust vs back squat EMG. Hip thrust = 69.5-86.8% mean glute max MVIC.
3. **Hegedus et al. 2022** (PMC8835508) — RDL vs step-RDL vs stiff-leg DL EMG. ST higher in RDL, glute max higher in SLDL.
4. **Martins-Costa et al. 2020** (PMC7046193) — Systematic review of deadlift EMG variants.
5. **Vigotsky et al. 2015** (PMC4304869) — Effects of load on good morning EMG.
6. **Baroni et al. 2024** (PubMed 39214520) — Reverse Nordic curl quadriceps EMG.
7. **PMC6350668** — Systematic review: glute max EMG during bodyweight hip extension exercises.
8. **PMC5534144** — Modified single-leg bridge EMG: 135-degree knee flex reduces hamstring compensation.
9. **PubMed 35586943** — Barbell hip thrust vs glute bridge: glute bridge = higher glute max, lower VL.
10. **PMC6709890** — Bulgarian squat EMG with vibration/suspension/unstable surface variations.
11. **Serner et al. 2013** (PubMed 23511698) — 8 hip adduction exercises EMG. Copenhagen = highest adductor longus.
12. **PMC8057707** — Roman chair vs RDL vs machine back extension: Roman chair = highest glute max + BF activation.
13. **PMC4492645** — Hamstring exercise classification by muscle and intensity. Sliding leg curl = highest intensity.
14. **PMC8219264** — Nordic hamstring curl: shallow knee angle increases BF activation.
15. **PubMed 34027916** — Hyperextension vs reverse hyperextension EMG differences.
16. **PMC7727410** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of glute med/min exercises.
17. **PMC9362892** — Proximal/distal BF and glute max during 6 hip extension/knee flexion exercises.
18. **PMC6063068** — Lunge variations EMG (forward vs lateral), adductor and glute med analysis.
